<details><summary>Other Fungal Diseases: recall the key points for overview.</summary>

Uncommonly encountered fungi can cause infection in infants and children with immunosuppression or other underlying conditions. Fungi can cause invasive mold infections, such as mucormycosis, fusariosis, scedosporiosis, and the phaeohyphomycoses (black molds), as well as invasive yeast infections with organisms such as Malazzesia, Trichosporon, Rhodotorula, and many more (more common mycoses, including aspergillosis, blastomycosis, candidiasis, coccidioidomycosis, cryptococcosis, histoplasmosis, paracoccidioidomycosis, and sporotrichosis, are discussed in individual chapters of Section 3 of the Red Book). Children can acquire infection from these fungi through inhalation via the respiratory tract or direct inoculation after traumatic disruption of cutaneous barriers.

<details><summary>Which underlying host settings predispose children to uncommon invasive fungal infections?</summary>

Immunosuppression and other underlying host conditions predispose children to these infections. Reversing severe neutropenia and reducing immunosuppression, where feasible, are important management considerations.

*Printed p. 263 / PDF p. 277*
</details>

<details><summary>How can children acquire uncommon invasive fungal infections?</summary>

Acquisition can follow inhalation through the respiratory tract or direct inoculation after disruption of the skin barrier.

<details><summary>Why should uncommon invasive fungal treatment not be reduced to a single universal drug rule?</summary>

Susceptibility data are limited, optimal treatments are not well established, and antifungal choice can depend on the particular species.

*Printed p. 263 / PDF p. 277*
</details>

<details><summary>What nonpharmacologic management measures are emphasized for invasive fungal infection?</summary>

Consider source control, reduction of immunosuppression, and surgical excision or debridement when feasible, together with specialist consultation.
