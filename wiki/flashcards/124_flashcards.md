<details><summary>Rickettsialpox: recall the key points for clinical manifestations.</summary>

Rickettsialpox is a febrile, eschar-associated illness characterized by generalized, relatively sparse, erythematous, papulovesicular eruptions on the trunk, face, extremities (less often on palms and soles), and occasionally involving the oral mucous membranes. The rash may resemble chickenpox and develops 1 to 4 days after onset of fever and 3 to 10 days after appearance of an eschar at the site of the bite of an infected house mouse mite. Eschars are generally 0.5 to 2.5 cm, are most frequently found on the extremities and torso, and may be nontender or mildly tender.

<details><summary>Rickettsialpox: recall the key points for etiology.</summary>

Rickettsialpox is caused by Rickettsia akari, a gram-negative intracellular bacillus now classified with Rickettsia felis and Rickettsia australis within the transitional group of the genus Rickettsia, so named because they have features of both the spotted fever and typhus groups.

<details><summary>Rickettsialpox: recall the key points for epidemiology.</summary>

The natural host for R akari in the United States is Mus musculus, the common house mouse. The organism is transmitted by the bite of the house mouse mite, Liponyssoides sanguineus. Disease risk is heightened in areas infested with house mice.

</details>

<details><summary>Rickettsialpox: recall the key points for diagnostic tests.</summary>

Serologic tests for antibodies reactive with spotted fever group rickettsial antigens are the most frequently used diagnostic assays. Immunoglobulin (Ig) M and IgG are generally detected 7 to 15 days after illness onset. R akari can be isolated in cell culture from blood and eschar biopsy specimens during the acute stage of disease, but culture is not attempted routinely.

</details>

<details><summary>Rickettsialpox: recall the key points for treatment.</summary>

Doxycycline is the drug of choice in all age groups. The minimum course of therapy is 5 days. Treatment is continued for at least 3 days after resolution of fever and until there is clinical improvement.

<details><summary>Rickettsialpox: recall the key points for infection prevention and control measures in health care settings.</summary>

Person-to-person spread of rickettsialpox has not been reported. Standard precautions are recommended.

<details><summary>Rickettsialpox: recall the key points for control measures.</summary>

Application of residual acaricides can be used in heavily mite-infested environments to eliminate the vector. Rodent-control measures are important in limiting or eliminating spread of rickettsialpox but they should be conducted only in conjunction with acaricide application to ensure vector control. No specific management of exposed people is necessary.
