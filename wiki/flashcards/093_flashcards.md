<details><summary>Mumps: recall the key points for clinical manifestations.</summary>

Mumps is a systemic disease typically characterized by pain and swelling of one or more of the salivary glands, usually the parotid glands. Nonspecific symptoms may precede parotitis, including fever, myalgia, anorexia, malaise, and headache. Approximately one-fifth of infections in unvaccinated people may be asymptomatic.

</details>

<details><summary>Mumps: recall the key points for etiology.</summary>

Mumps is an enveloped RNA virus with 12 genotypes in the genus Rubulavirus in the family Paramyxoviridae. The genus also includes human parainfluenza virus types 2 and 4. Other common infectious causes of parotitis include Epstein-Barr virus, adenovirus, human herpes virus 6, parainfluenza virus types 1 and 3, influenza A virus, enteroviruses, HIV, nontuberculous mycobacterium, and gram-positive (eg, Staphylococcus aureus) and gram-negative bacteria.

*Printed p. 593 / PDF p. 607*
</details>

<details><summary>Mumps: recall the key points for epidemiology.</summary>

Mumps occurs worldwide, and humans are the only known natural hosts. The virus is spread by contact with infectious respiratory tract secretions and saliva. Mumps virus is the only known cause of epidemic parotitis.

*Printed p. 593 / PDF p. 607*
</details>

<details><summary>Mumps: recall the key points for diagnostic tests.</summary>

Unvaccinated and vaccinated people with parotitis, orchitis, or oophoritis without other apparent cause should undergo diagnostic testing for mumps virus. Mumps can be confirmed by detection of mumps virus nucleic acid by nucleic acid amplification tests (NAATs) in buccal swab specimens (Stensen duct exudates), throat or oral swab specimens, urine, or cerebrospinal fluid. The parotid gland should be massaged for 30 seconds prior to buccal swab specimen collection.

*Printed p. 594 / PDF p. 608*
</details>

<details><summary>Mumps: recall the key points for infection prevention and control measures in health care settings.</summary>

In addition to standard precautions, droplet precautions are recommended until 5 days after onset of parotid swelling.

</details>

<details><summary>Mumps: recall the key points for diagnostic tests — additional detail.</summary>

Samples for mumps virus detection should be obtained soon after clinical diagnosis of mumps, preferably within 1 to 3 days after symptom onset. Vaccinated individuals may shed virus for a shorter period (less than 1 week) and may shed smaller amounts of virus.
