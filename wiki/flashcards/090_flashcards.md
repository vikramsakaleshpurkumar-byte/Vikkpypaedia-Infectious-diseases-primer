<details><summary>Molluscum Contagiosum: recall the key points for clinical manifestations.</summary>

Molluscum contagiosum is a benign viral infection of the skin with no systemic manifestations. It usually is characterized by 1 to 20 discrete, 2- to 5-mm-diameter, flesh-colored to translucent, dome-shaped papules, some with central umbilication. Lesions commonly occur on the trunk, face, and extremities but rarely are generalized.

<details><summary>Molluscum Contagiosum: recall the key points for etiology.</summary>

Molluscum contagiosum virus is the sole member of the genus Molluscipoxvirus, family Poxviridae. DNA subtypes of molluscum contagiosum virus can be differentiated, but the specific subtype probably is insignificant in pathogenesis. Other poxviruses include the agents of smallpox, mpox, vaccinia, and cowpox.

<details><summary>Molluscum Contagiosum: recall the key points for epidemiology.</summary>

Humans are the only known source of the virus, which is spread by direct contact with lesions, including scratching, shaving, sexual contact, or fomites. Vertical transmission has been linked with neonatal molluscum contagiosum infection. Lesions can be disseminated by autoinoculation.

<details><summary>Molluscum Contagiosum: recall the key points for diagnostic tests.</summary>

The diagnosis usually can be made clinically from the characteristic appearance of umbilicated papules. Side lighting of the individual lesions may enhance the visibility of the umbilication. Handheld dermoscopy can be useful in identifying orifices (umbilications) along with white amorphous structures with a surrounding crown of vessels.

<details><summary>Molluscum Contagiosum: recall the key points for treatment.</summary>

There is no consensus on management of molluscum contagiosum in children and adolescents. Genital lesions in sexually active patients should be treated to prevent spread to sexual contacts. Nongenital lesions in healthy people are typically self-limited, so treatment may be unnecessary.

<details><summary>Molluscum Contagiosum: recall the key points for control measures.</summary>

No control measures are known for isolated cases. For outbreaks, which are common in the tropics, restricting direct person-to-person contact and sharing of potentially contaminated fomites, such as towels and bedding, may decrease spread. Molluscum contagiosum should not prevent a child from attending child care or school or from swimming in public pools.

<details><summary>Molluscum Contagiosum: recall the key points for treatment — additional detail.</summary>

Physical destruction of the lesions is the most rapid and effective means of curing molluscum contagiosum. Modalities available include curettage, cryodestruction with liquid nitrogen, electrodesiccation, and chemical agents designed to initiate a local inflammatory response (podophyllin, tretinoin, cantharidin, 25%–50% trichloroacetic acid, liquefied phenol, silver nitrate, tincture of iodine, or potassium hydroxide).
