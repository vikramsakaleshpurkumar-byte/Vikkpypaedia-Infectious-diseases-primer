Group B streptococci are a major cause of perinatal infections, including urinary tract infection, bacteremia, intra-amniotic infection (formerly called chorioamnionitis), and endometritis in pregnant and postpartum people, as well as systemic and focal infections in neonates and young infants. In newborn infants, early-onset disease (EOD) usually occurs within the first 24 hours after birth (range, 0 through 6 days), presenting with respiratory distress, apnea, pneumonia, shock and less often, meningitis (5%–10% of cases). Late-onset disease (LOD), which typically occurs at 3 to 4 weeks of age (range, 7 through 89 days), commonly manifests as bacteremia without a focus (60%–65% of cases) or meningitis (approximately 30% of cases); other focal infections, often with concomitant bacteremia, include osteomyelitis, septic arthritis, necrotizing fasciitis, pneumonia, adenitis, and cellulitis.
